When two bodies touch over a patch and not at a point, which contact model gets the physics right?

Conforming contact (a shaft in a bearing, a peg in a hole, a block resting on a face) is where point-contact models are least reliable. This project compares two elastic-foundation laws, evaluated on the exact intersection of triangle meshes, with the native contact of Drake, Chrono and MuJoCo, on a battery of {{scenarios}} scenarios whose answer is known analytically.

The work was carried out by an AI system (Claude Fable 5.2, Anthropic) directed and supervised by David Vilela.

The laws live in an engine-independent C++20 library. Every number on this page comes from a run you can replay: pick a scenario and watch each engine against the theory.

What the evidence does not cover

There is no experimental reference: the references are analytic, and class C tests the consistency of the foundation model, not its accuracy against reality. A static finite-element solution exists for two class B scenarios: it confirms the foundation's pressure for a thin sleeve, and shows that on a thick slab the foundation itself, and with it every model built on it, Drake's hydroelastic contact included, misses the stop time of a spinning disk. The coupling with the engines is explicit, which limits very stiff contacts. The full list of open problems is in the README.
