# Scenario battery: specification

Scenarios with a closed-form (or exactly integrable) theoretical result,
run with three contact methods (`gonthier`, `distributed`, `native`) on Drake
and Chrono. This file is the contract between the mesh generator, the scenario
API, the engine backends and the scenario implementations.

## 1. Mesh assets

Generated by `tools/meshgen.py` (surfaces and convex pieces) and
`tools/volume_mesh.py` (tetrahedral meshes); outputs are committed in `data/` (see `data/README.md`).

### Criteria (every asset)

1. Files in metres; names in millimetres: `<kind>_<params>`, decimals allowed
   (`hole_s99.8_d100`). Curved assets end in `_seg<N>`, the number of segments
   per full turn: N = 32 (coarse), 64 (base) and 128 (fine). All three are generated.
2. Closed, manifold, triangles only, outward (counter-clockwise seen from
   outside), no duplicate vertices, no degenerate triangles, positive volume.
   Coordinates written with 17 significant digits, so dimensions are exact.
3. Meshes approximate the solid from inside: vertices of convex curved
   surfaces lie on the true surface; the polygonal surface of a concave curved
   surface (cavity) encloses the true cavity (facets tangent to it where the
   tessellation allows, never inside it). A body and a cavity of the same
   nominal size therefore never interfere spuriously.
4. Curved surfaces use the angular step 2π/N in every curved direction (UV
   spheres: N longitudes, N/2 latitude bands); partial surfaces round their
   span to whole steps.
5. Each asset is a folder `data/bodies/<name>/` (box, cylinder, sphere,
   frustum, ring) or `data/grounds/<name>/` (the rest), holding the same files:
   `surface.obj` is the whole closed surface. A non-convex asset also has
   `pieces/NNN.obj`: convex pieces with disjoint interiors whose union is
   exactly the solid; each passes `CollisionShape::convex()`.
6. `volume.vtk` (every asset except slabs, written by `tools/volume_mesh.py`
   when meshgen runs; generated, not committed): tetrahedral mesh of the solid
   in Drake's legacy VTK format, fine enough that Drake's pressure field
   p = E·φ/H (φ = distance to the boundary, H = max φ over vertices) is linear
   in depth near the faces. Its title line (line 2) is
   `volcontact foundation_depth=<H>` with H in metres, computed exactly as Drake
   does (max over vertices of the distance to the boundary surface).

### Frames

- Bodies (`box`, `cylinder`, `sphere`, `frustum`, `ring`): origin at the
  centroid of the true solid, axis of revolution along z. `frustum_r<a>_r<b>`:
  radius a at the bottom (z < 0). Suffix `_o<x>x<y>x<z>`: the origin is moved to
  that point (mm) relative to the centroid.
- `slab`: top face z = 0, centred in x and y.
- `table_<L>x<W>x<H>[_p<N>]`: a finite box with its top face at z = 0, centred in x and y. Unlike
  a slab it has a `volume.vtk`. With `_p<N>` (N a power of two, 2^k) it is cut into
  2^⌈k/2⌉ × 2^⌊k/2⌋ equal tiles along x and y, one piece each.
- `vgroove_w<W>_l<L>`: 90° V along x, bottom line on the x axis, faces
  z = |y| up to the rim |y| = W/2, material down to z = −100 mm, x ∈ ±L/2.
  Two pieces.
- `hole_s<S>_d<D>`: square through-hole of side S centred on the z axis, top
  at z = 0, depth D, walls 100 mm thick. Four wall pieces.
- `corner_s<S>`: floor (top z = 0) and walls with faces x = 0 and y = 0,
  covering [0, S] in x, y, z; thickness 50 mm. Three disjoint box pieces:
  floor x, y ∈ [−50, S], z ∈ [−50, 0]; wall x ∈ [−50, 0], y ∈ [−50, S],
  z ∈ [0, S]; wall x ∈ [0, S], y ∈ [−50, 0], z ∈ [0, S].
- `bowl_r<R>_t<T>_a<A>_n<N>`: spherical cavity of radius R from the bottom to
  polar angle A (degrees), shell thickness T along the facet normals. Origin at
  the lowest point of the true cavity, sphere centre at (0, 0, R). One piece per
  facet.
- `trough_r<R>_t<T>_a<A>_l<L>_n<N>`: cylindrical cavity of radius R, axis along
  y, spanning ±A degrees from the bottom, length L. Origin on the bottom line of
  the true cavity, axis at z = R. One piece per facet strip.
- `bearing_r<R>_t<T>_l<L>_n<N>`: full cylindrical hole of radius R, axis along
  y through the origin, length L, wall T. One piece per facet strip.
- `seat_r<a>_r<b>_h<H>_t<T>_n<N>`: conical cavity matching
  `frustum_r<a>_r<b>_h<H>`, extended 10 mm beyond both ends along the cone, open
  at the bottom. Origin where the frustum's centroid is when seated with zero
  interference. One piece per facet strip.

## 2. Scenario API

- `ShapeMeshes { surface, pieces, volume }` and
  `shape_meshes(data_dir, name)`: `surface.obj`; pieces `pieces/*.obj` or
  `{surface}`; `volume.vtk` if present, in `bodies/<name>/` or `grounds/<name>/`.
- `BodySpec::shape` (in the body frame), `BodySpec::applied_force`
  (`std::function<Vec3(double t)>`, world force at the centre of mass, empty if
  none). `GroundSpec::shape`, `GroundSpec::planar` (top is the world plane
  z = 0; native contact may use a half-space).
- `ContactSpec::friction`: Coulomb μ of the pair for native contact.
  `NativeContactSpec { stiffness, damping }` per scenario.
- `Scenario::theory_class` (`TheoryClass::kA`, `kB`, `kC`; `to_string` gives "A", "B", "C"):
  what the theory rests on (§4.1).
- `MetricReport::error`: the worst normalized deviation of every quantity the theory
  fixes (dimensionless, ≥ 0; §4.1); the statistics use it. Its terms are the scalars
  `err_<name>`. Scalars hold the measured value and its theory as `<x>` and `<x>_theory`; a
  class B scenario adds its band (`<x>_<distribution>`, `<x>_band_min`, `<x>_band_max`,
  `band_error`), and a scenario with a Winkler-specific side prediction adds `<x>_winkler`.
- `ScenarioOptions { data_dir, resolution, stiffness_scale }`, `segments()` = 32, 64 or 128
  (`Resolution::kCoarse`, `kBase`, `kFine`); `stiffness_scale` multiplies the stiffness of the
  gonthier and distributed contacts and `native.stiffness` (damping unchanged).
  Catalog: `catalog()` lists the factories by name; `make_scenario(name, o)`;
  `depends_on_resolution(name)` is false for scenarios whose surfaces at base and fine are the same
  (no mesh series for them).
- Variants (`variants()`): `base` (N = 64, dt 1 ms); `mesh32`, `mesh128` (curved scenarios only);
  `dt2`, `dt0.5`, `dt0.2`, `dt0.1` (step in ms); `stiff` (K × 10 at dt 0.2 ms, which keeps K·dt²
  in range; not run for class C, whose theory depends on K); `perturbed1` … `perturbed8` (every
  dynamic body gets a velocity and angular-velocity kick of 1 mm/s and 0.01 rad/s along
  directions drawn from a seeded `mt19937_64`, Box–Muller, one stream per seed; `perturb(s, seed)`).
  They form three convergence series (step, mesh, stiffness) and one perturbation set; there is no
  median over variants any more: the statistics use `base` and read the others as series.
- Methods in the bench: `gonthier`, `gonthier-thesis` (the same law with the friction model of the
  2018 thesis, `friction_model = kThesis2018`; set by the bench on a copy of the scenario),
  `distributed` and the native models (`native`, `native-<model>`). `--contact` and `--variant`
  take comma lists; `--time-limit S` aborts a run after S wall seconds (`volcontact::RunTimeout`; Drake
  checks every 100 steps) and records it as `timeout`; `--resume` skips the rows of the CSV with
  status ok; `--terms FILE` writes the terms of every error.
- Battery CSV (`volcontact_bench --csv`): `engine,contact,scenario,variant,status,error,realtime_ratio,wall_s,steps,dt,segments,stiffness_scale,class`.
  `status` is `ok`, `nan`, `timeout` or `failed`; `error` and `realtime_ratio` are empty for the
  last two; `segments` is 0 for scenarios without curved assets. Terms CSV (`results/terms.csv`):
  `scenario,engine,contact,variant,term,value`.
- Statistics (`tools/stats.py`, docs/research/scenario-audit.md §4): pass rates at 1/5/20 % per
  theory class; paired log10 error ratios (distributed vs gonthier, thesis vs gonthier, each law vs
  each native of its engine) with a stratified bootstrap (scenarios resampled within class) on
  A, A+B and C; performance profiles (A+B all methods; C laws and Winkler natives); convergence
  slopes against dt and mesh with flags (converging, flat, diverging, non-monotone, failed); the
  stiffness series; engine independence; spread over the eight perturbations. Errors below 1e-3
  are ties; nan, timeout and failed count as failures. Class C is compared only among laws and
  Drake's hydroelastic (the only Winkler-type native). Outputs: `results/stats.md`, `stats.json`,
  `results/figures`.

## 3. Native contact (generic rules)

One generic configuration per native model, the same for every scenario and
never tuned to one: the one each engine's developers document as competent use.
The sources are listed at the end of the section.

**Contract.** `Engine::native_models()` lists an engine's native contact models,
its default first: Drake `hydroelastic`, `point`; Chrono `smc`, `nsc`; MuJoCo
`soft`, `default`. `RunOptions::native_model` selects one (empty: the default; an unknown
name throws `std::invalid_argument`). `volcontact_bench --contact native` runs
the default, `--contact native:<model>` that model, `--contact native:all`
every model, and `--list-native` prints them. The CSV `contact` column (and the
trajectory file name) is `native` for the default and `native-<model>` for the
others; `tools/stats.py` and the viewer read the three methods `gonthier`,
`distributed`, `native` and ignore the extra rows. A recorded native run reports
`RunStats::native_contact_points`, the mean number of contact points per step.

**Reference area.** Models whose stiffness belongs to a contact point (N/m)
have no areal stiffness to map K (N/m³) onto. They use the reference area of a
solid, A = a sixth of its surface area (the face of a cube, the mean face of a
box), so that K·A is the stiffness of a Winkler foundation K under a face of
that size. It sets the scale; it is exact only when the contact is a face of
area A. Used by Drake `point` and Chrono `smc`.

### Drake `hydroelastic` (default)

- Planar grounds are the compliant half-space (E = K·1 m, 1 m slab); other
  grounds are compliant `volume.vtk` meshes. Bodies are rigid unless a contact
  pairs them with another rigid body: the contact graph is 2-coloured from the
  grounds (compliant), and a non-bipartite graph is an error. A compliant mesh
  gets E = K·H (H from the VTK title), so its pressure is K times the depth; a
  compliant primitive (`native_primitives`) gets E = K·H with H its own
  foundation depth (box: smallest half side; sphere: radius; cylinder:
  min(radius, half length)).
- **A rigid body that touches a half-space is its `surface.obj` refined until
  no edge exceeds 1/8 of the mesh's largest side** (every longer edge is split
  at its midpoint, pass after pass; done in memory when the body is registered).
  Against a tetrahedral mesh the plain `surface.obj` is enough: the tetrahedra
  already cut the contact surface finely, and refining only costs time. Drake turns each polygon of
  a contact surface into one contact point, and its guide warns that "half
  spaces are not represented by meshes ... the contact surface's refinement will
  depend on the other geometry's level of refinement" and that "getting moments
  out of the contact depends on how many elements are in the contact surface"
  [D1]. The bottom face of a 12-triangle box is two triangles: on the half-space
  it has two contact points on one diagonal and no stiffness about it; refined,
  it has 100 to 500.
- Per-geometry μ is solved so that Drake's combination 2μᵢμⱼ/(μᵢ+μⱼ) gives each
  pair's μ; an inconsistent cycle is an error. Hunt–Crossley dissipation d = h on
  every geometry, discrete contact approximation `kLagged` (it keeps the
  Hunt–Crossley form [D1]), Drake's default margin. Pairs that no `ContactSpec`
  declares are filtered out.
- Why this assignment and this resolution (single runs, base variant, 1 ms;
  error, and wall time over simulated time):

  | Scenario | 12 triangles | edge ≤ L/8 | edge ≤ L/16 | edge ≤ L/32 | rigid ground, compliant `volume.vtk` body |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | `block` | 13 %, 0.09 | 1.4 %, 0.7 | 1.4 %, 2.3 | 1.4 %, 9 | 0.15 %, 7 |
  | `offset` | 113 %, 0.08 | 0.46 %, 1.0 | 0.11 %, 3.5 | 0.02 %, 13 | 11 %, 21 |
  | `push` | 4.3 %, 0.09 | 1.7 %, 1.1 | 1.7 %, 3.8 | 1.7 %, 13 | 0.003 %, 10 |
  | `tip` | 2.0 %, 0.08 | 1.6 %, 0.3 | 0.1 %, 0.8 | 1.8 %, 2.1 | 2.7 %, 3.7 |
  | `twoblocks` | 2.0 %, 38 | 0.8 %, 25 | 0.8 %, 37 | 0.8 %, 84 | 0.05 %, 44 |
  | `vgroove` | 0.8 %, 5 | | | | 0.3 %, 81 |
  | `hole` | 87 %, 9 | | | | 52 %, 22 |
  | `corner` | 9.5 %, 16 | | | | 26 %, 51 |

  L/8 is the coarsest refinement that removes the two-point artefact ("the
  coarsest mesh that produces desirable results will allow the simulation to run
  as efficiently as possible" [D1]); finer meshes change `offset` only and cost
  four times more per halving. The opposite assignment (rigid grounds, compliant
  bodies, as in Drake's hydroelastic tutorial) is better on sliding boxes and
  worse on statics, and 5 to 20 times slower with the 50 000-tetrahedron
  `volume.vtk` meshes, so it was not adopted.

### Drake `point`

- Drake's compliant point contact [D2], same plant, solver (`kLagged`),
  dissipation d = h and friction rule as above. It acts between convex shapes
  and reports one point per pair of geometries, so a body is its analytic
  primitive when the scenario gives one (always, not only with
  `native_primitives`) and otherwise its convex pieces (`Convex`); a planar
  ground is the half-space and any other ground its convex pieces.
- Point stiffness of a geometry: k = K·A, A its reference area. Drake combines
  two geometries in series, k₁k₂/(k₁+k₂) [D2], so the smaller body sets the
  pair's stiffness.
- A box also carries a sphere of radius 1e-7 m on each corner, as Drake's own
  box models do (the foam brick of its manipulation station [D3]): one point per
  pair cannot hold a face ("contact along a large interface is treated as
  contact at a single point" [D2]). No such device exists for the flat face of
  a cylinder, which keeps its single point.

### Chrono `smc` (default)

- **Shapes.** A body is its analytic primitive when the scenario gives one
  (box, cylinder, sphere), otherwise its convex pieces; a ground is its convex
  pieces. A piece that is a box (8 vertices at the corners of a box) is a box
  shape, any other a convex hull. Chrono's manual: concave shapes "fall back on
  a decomposition of the geometry in a union of convex shapes" [C1]. Boxes and
  cylinders against boxes give manifolds of 3–4 points; a sphere gives one.
  Triangle-mesh grounds were tried and rejected (bodies pass through `hole` and
  `corner`, `trough` ejects the cylinder). Envelope 1 mm and margin 0.5 mm, the
  values of the manual's example [C1]; they change nothing measurable here.
- **Force law.** Hooke with explicit coefficients (`UseMaterialProperties(false)`),
  set per pair in the contact callback together with μ. Chrono's manual: "the
  contact stiffness is an artificial term, usually empirically related to the
  material stiffness" [C1]. Normal stiffness per contact point
  kn = K·A/4, A the smaller reference area of the two bodies: a face contact has
  four manifold points and then the stiffness K·A. Tangential stiffness kt = kn,
  as in Chrono's own material-based Hooke law [C3]. Normal damping gn = h·|g|
  (Chrono multiplies it by the pair's effective mass): the Hunt–Crossley term
  K·A·δ·h·δ̇ linearized at the static penetration under the body's weight. A
  fixed body counts with infinite mass (1e12 kg) in the effective mass, not with
  `ChBody`'s default 1 kg.
- **It is explicit, so the step is subdivided.** `SetContactStiff(true)` has no
  effect in Chrono 10: the Jacobians of the contact force are commented out in
  its source [C3], whatever the solver. Chrono's manual says of SMC that
  "shorter timesteps are required, especially for harder contact stiffness"
  [C1]. The backend takes n equal sub-steps per step dt, the smallest n with
  ω·dt/n ≤ 0.1, ω² = K·A/m_eff the largest over the pairs (at least 60 steps
  per contact oscillation). In the battery n is 1 to 15 at dt = 1 ms; the
  trajectory is still sampled every dt.
- **Stick is viscous.** Chrono 10's tangential force below the Coulomb limit is
  (kt·Δt + gt·m_eff)·v per point: its multi-step tangential displacement is not
  implemented and uses the one-step value v·Δt [C3], so there is no stiction
  memory and a held body creeps.
  gt = 1/(16·Δt), Δt the sub-step: a full manifold then takes a quarter of the
  pair's momentum per sub-step, the stiffest stick an explicit step integrates
  without ringing. The creep speed under a tangential load a_t per unit mass is
  below 4·a_t·Δt.

### Chrono `nsc`

- Chrono's complementarity (rigid) contact, `ChSystemNSC`, with the shapes of
  `smc`, the pair's μ as static and sliding friction, restitution 0, no
  compliance: K and h do not enter. Time stepper `EULER_IMPLICIT_LINEARIZED`
  (Chrono's default, "works for DVI contacts"), solver Barzilai–Borwein with 200
  iterations: the manual recommends it for unilateral constraints when accuracy
  matters [C2]. APGD with 200 or 1000 iterations left the `stack` jittering
  apart and was rejected. Maximum penetration recovery speed: Chrono's default.

### MuJoCo `soft` (default)

MuJoCo has one contact model: soft constraints at contact points between convex
geoms, solved as a convex problem [M4]. `soft` is that model as its manual
recommends configuring it [M1], with the scenario's K mapped onto the stiffness.

- **Shapes.** A body is its analytic primitive when the scenario gives one
  (box, cylinder, sphere; always, not only with `native_primitives`), otherwise
  one mesh geom per convex piece, which MuJoCo collides as its convex hull; a
  planar ground is a plane, any other its convex pieces. The manual: "replacing
  collision meshes with primitives improves both speed and contact normals",
  and where meshes are unavoidable, "convex-decompose them" [M1]. Contacts come
  from explicit pairs, one per combination of geoms of the two bodies of every
  `ContactSpec`, so each pair has its own μ. Native convex collision with
  multiple contacts (`nativeccd`, `multiccd`, both MuJoCo's defaults [M3]):
  4 points for a box or a mesh face, 3 for the cap of a cylinder on a plane, 2
  for a cylinder on its side, 1 for a sphere. Margin and gap 0.
- **Options**, the "recommended initial values" of the manual's tuning table
  [M1]: integrator `implicitfast`, elliptic cones, `impratio` 10. Three no-slip
  iterations ("for residual slow slippage, `noslip_iterations` of 1–3" [M1]):
  without them a stack under a tangential load creeps (`stack` 0.2 → 11 %).
  Newton solver with tolerance 1e-12 and no-slip tolerance 1e-10 ("it should be
  used with aggressive tolerance values, say 1e-10" [M2]): with the default 1e-8
  the corners of a sliding box get unequal forces and `block` at dt = 0.2 ms
  ends 9 % fast; 1e-10 is not enough, 1e-12 gives 0.08 %.
- **Reference area.** The pair gets A_ref = the smaller of its two geoms'
  reference areas (a sixth of the surface, as for Drake `point` and Chrono
  `smc`, here per geom; a plane counts as infinite), so a body made of many
  pieces has the stiffness of the pieces that touch. Each point of the manifold
  stands for a patch A = A_ref / 4; a sphere's single point for A = A_ref.
- **Stiffness from K.** With a constant impedance d0 (`solimp` = d0, d0) and
  the direct `solref` = (−k, −b), which the manual recommends for system
  identification [M2], MuJoCo gives a contact point the normal acceleration
  a1 = (1 − d0)·a0 + k·δ − b·δ̇ (a0 without the contact, δ the penetration). At
  rest the point carries the force k·δ·m_ref / (1 − d0), m_ref the reduced mass
  of the pair (grounds are infinite; MuJoCo's own estimate of the constraint's
  inverse inertia [M5]). Equating it to the Winkler patch K·A·δ gives
  k = (1 − d0)·K·A / m_ref, with d0 = 0.9, MuJoCo's default ("keep" [M1]). k is
  limited to 1/(2·dt)², the stiffest reference `refsafe` accepts (a log line
  says so: `billiard` and `sphere` at 1 ms). The static penetration of a flat
  contact is m·g / (K·A_ref), exact when A_ref is the contact area (a cube on
  a face). Only the statics are a spring's: the contact force is
  (1 − d0)·K·A·δ plus the share d0 of whatever load presses the bodies
  together, so an impact is slower and deeper than the foundation's.
- **Damping.** b = 2√k, critical ("would normally be set to 1" [M2]). The
  scenario's h does not apply: the damper is linear, so its restitution does not
  depend on the impact speed as Hunt–Crossley's does. h linearized at the
  static load (ζ = h·|g| / 2ω₀, ω₀² = K·A_ref / m_ref, the rule of Chrono `smc`)
  gives ζ = 0.02 to 0.25, and an underdamped contact bounces on the kicks
  described below (`block` 0.4 → 5 %, `drop` 44 → 132 %).
- **Friction.** condim 4: μ in both tangential directions and a torsional
  coefficient (2/3)·μ·√(A/π), a disk of area A under uniform pressure (the
  coefficient is a length, a contact-patch radius [M1][M3]); with condim 3
  the number of points between the cubes of `stack` changes during the run
  (box–box returns up to 8) and its compressions are off by 26 %. No rolling friction (condim 6 is for "rolling props that
  should settle" [M1]). The friction rows keep MuJoCo's default reference
  (`solreffriction` = 0.02 s, 1).
- **What the model does, whatever the configuration.**
  - *A sliding contact pushes the bodies apart.* MuJoCo's convex cone has no
    complementarity: "the only way to initiate slip is to generate some motion
    in the normal direction" [M4]. The normal force of a sliding point grows
    with μ·b_f·v (b_f the friction rows' reference damping, v the sliding
    speed), so a sliding box hops tens to hundreds of µm at 1 ms and touches
    the ground only part of the steps; the mean friction stays μ times the mean normal force. A
    stiffer friction reference hops more and loses friction on landing (`push`
    0.9 % at 20 ms, 8 % at 5 ms, 13 % at 2·dt), which is why the default is kept.
    A journal in a hole with no clearance has nowhere to go: the points all
    around it load each other and `bearing` stops eight times too early. Among
    the points of a spinning face the fastest, outermost ones take more load.
  - *A face is the points of its boundary.* The friction torque of a spinning
    flat contact is that of its rim (μ·N·R against (2/3)·μ·N·R for a disk) and
    its tilt stiffness is that of springs at its corners.
  - *Interference is resolved along the shortest way out*, and a facet is a
    rigid plane: a press fit is not entered, a ball in a faceted bowl rolls on
    the facets.
  - A failure of the step (bad state, full contact or constraint buffers) ends
    the run with an error.

### MuJoCo `default`

- What a user gets without touching anything: the shapes and explicit pairs of
  `soft`, the pair's μ, and MuJoCo's own values for the rest [M1]: `solref`
  = (0.02 s, 1), `solimp` = (0.9, 0.95, 0.001, 0.5, 2), condim 3, pyramidal
  cones, `impratio` 1, no no-slip pass, Newton with tolerance 1e-8, integrator
  `Euler`. K and h do not enter: the penetration at rest is the same for every
  K. The manual calls several of these "unfortunate defaults ... not what we
  recommend for new models today" [M1].

## 4. Scenarios

Common to all: g = 9.81 m/s²; inclines tilt gravity, (g sin θ, 0, −g cos θ),
downhill +x (a tilt that must be quasi-static is ramped by an applied force instead, since a step load topples tall bodies); planar ground `slab_40000x40000x1000`; damping h = 5 s/m (0.5 s/m in the impacts and 0 in `energy` of §4.3); μ_static = μ_dynamic = μ. Gonthier: bristle
defaults with the scenario's μ, friction at the volume centroid, μ_spin = μ.
Distributed: defaults with the scenario's μ. Stiction (both laws, the same for
every scenario): k = μ_s·|Fn| / d, c = k·τ, with d = 1e-4 m (displacement at the
static limit) and τ = 5 ms; the thesis's constant 400 N/m is a selectable mode. Masses are the ones given
(1 kg unless stated) with the inertia of the true solid. A run "stops" when its speed falls below 1 % of the initial one.

### 4.1 Rules

**Parameters are chosen before any run**, from the physics and from what the meshes and the
step can resolve, and written here. A scenario is never changed because a method scores badly on it.

**Initial conditions do not belong to any contact model.** A body that starts in contact
penetrates 1 µm, whatever the method (it keeps every engine away from vertices lying exactly on the
contact plane); none starts at a law's static penetration. The body then settles on its contact
during the first milliseconds. That does not change the events measured: the friction impulse up
to time t is μ·∫N dt = μ·m·(g_n·t + v_n(t) − v_n(0)), which is the rigid one as soon as the normal
velocity has died out, so stop times, sliding velocities and the rolling onset are unaffected;
ramps (`push`, `tip`, `stack`) reach their event seconds after the settling; the rolling scenarios
(`cylinder`, `sphere`) measure after a settling interval of 0.2 s. The exceptions are part of the
scenario: `hole` starts with its interference (it is what the scenario is about) and `bearing`
concentric in a hole of its own radius.

**Class of the theory** (`Scenario::theory_class`, column `class` of the battery CSV, `"class"` in
the JSON outputs, third field in `tools/viewer/scenarios.js`):

- **A**: a rigid-body result that does not depend on the contact model. Any model must converge
  to it as it stiffens. Only these scenarios rank a volumetric law against a contact model of
  another kind.
- **B**: the theory assumes a pressure distribution (uniform, the one a rigid body produces on a
  Winkler foundation). `error` is measured against that nominal answer; the answers of the other
  admissible distributions are scalars (`<x>_<distribution>`), with the band they span
  (`<x>_band_min`, `<x>_band_max`) and `band_error`, the distance of the measured value to the
  band over the nominal value (0 inside). A method inside the band is not wrong. For `disk` and
  `bearing` a static finite-element solution (`tools/fem`, `results/fem/README.md`) places a
  continuum support in the band: the rigid-punch end for `disk`'s thick slab, the cosine for
  `bearing`'s thin sleeve. It is not the scenarios' reference; no scenario has a measured one.
- **C**: Winkler consistency. The theory contains K or integrates the law's own force
  (penetrations, tilts, the law's impact ODE). It says whether a method is a Winkler foundation
  of stiffness K, not whether it is accurate.

**`error` is the worst normalized deviation of every quantity the theory fixes**, not only the
main result. Each term is the scalar `err_<name>`, so a table can show which one dominates:

- a value with a non-zero theory: |measured − theory| / |theory|;
- a length the theory keeps at zero (creep, slip, slide, lateral shift): the length over a length
  of the body (the side along the motion);
- an amplitude: the change over the initial amplitude;
- a residual speed in a scenario that must end at rest: the mean speed over the last window times
  the duration of the run (the drift it would add up to), over a length of the body;
- a rotation the theory keeps at zero: the displacement of the body's end, angle·L/2, over L.

A term that the run did not produce (a body that never stopped, never tipped) is NaN and makes
`error` NaN. Relative errors of different scenarios are not commensurable (their sensitivity to μ
goes from 0.5 to 6.5): compare methods within a scenario.

Notes on the scenarios whose design needs a reason:

- **`cylinder`, `sphere`, `billiard` (what the rolling theory covers).** A Winkler pressure with
  damping, K·ℓ·(1 − h·v_n), is larger on the leading half of a rolling patch than on the trailing
  half. It resists the rolling with M = N·h·ω·a²/k, with a the half-width of the patch (a² = 2rδ)
  and k = 6 for a spherical cap, 5 for a cylinder. So v̇ = a₀ − c·v with
  c = N·h·a²/(k·m·r²·(1 + β)), β = I/(mr²). This term is the law's own (it has h, and K through a),
  is absent from the rigid theory and vanishes at rest. The scenarios therefore measure what the
  rigid theory fixes whatever that term is: the acceleration at zero speed (`cylinder`, `sphere`)
  and the rolling onset (`billiard`). The term is reported apart: `rolling_resistance_rate`
  (measured, −2p₂/p₁) next to `rolling_resistance_rate_winkler` (c predicted for the Winkler laws:
  0.0235 s⁻¹ in `cylinder`, 0.0595 s⁻¹ in `sphere`), and `velocity_end` next to the rigid
  `velocity_end_theory` and the Winkler `velocity_end_winkler` = (a₀/c)(1 − e^(−cT)). What is left in
  `err_acceleration` is not rolling resistance of that form: it is a resistance that does not
  vanish at rest (Coulomb-like rolling friction, stiction residue) or an error of the inertia or
  the normal. A final velocity below the rigid one by c·T/2 is the law's damping, to be judged as a
  model property (it goes to zero with h or as K grows), not as an error against this theory.
- **`bowl`, `trough` (resolution).** R − r = 200 mm: the penetration (0.46 mm in `bowl`) and the sag
  of the 64-segment meshes (0.36 mm for the bowl's facets, 0.12 mm for the sphere) change the
  pendulum's length by a few tenths of a percent, where the previous design (R − r = 5 mm) could
  not tell them from the period it measured. The contact travels 240 mm of arc per swing, eight
  29 mm facets, and the patch (radius 12 mm) is smaller than a facet. The amplitude 0.4 rad is not
  small, so the period is the exact one of the pendulum (arithmetic–geometric mean). The amplitude
  is part of the error. The Winkler rolling resistance above damps the swing at
  γ = N·h·a²/(2k·m·r²·(1 + β)) with a² = 2δ·rR/(R − r); the scalar `amplitude_last_winkler` is
  θ₀·e^(−γt) at the time of the last extreme (0.88 θ₀ in `bowl`), so that a loss of amplitude can be
  split into the law's damping and the rest.
- **`journal` and `bearing`.** A journal without clearance is statically indeterminate: the
  friction torque depends on the pressure the compliance model produces, so `bearing` is class B.
  With clearance the contact is a line and the torque is μmgr/√(1 + μ²) for any model: `journal`,
  class A. Its clearance (10 mm) and stiffness follow from resolution: the half-angle of the
  contact arc is 0.26 rad, so a parabolic pressure gives 1 + φ²/10 = 1.007 times the line torque
  (scalar `stop_time_winkler_arc`), and the penetration (0.3 mm) is larger than the sag of the two
  polygons (0.13 mm), whose facets pass under the contact. The shaft starts where the theory
  keeps it, so that no transient of the climb is measured.
- **`corner`.** With friction the statics are indeterminate (any wall force in a wide interval is
  an equilibrium, and where the cube ends depends on the path), so μ = 0; the penetrations are
  then the law's (class C).
- **`push`.** The onset is the time from which the box keeps moving, so that a passing transient
  above 1 mm/s is not taken for it; creep before the onset is a term of its own.

Why these parameters (decided before the runs):

- **`pieces`.** The seams of every decomposition include x = 0, which both boxes cross; the box
  that slides is yawed so that no edge of it is parallel to a seam. The whole ground is in the
  same run, so the comparison needs no theory and `pieces1` is the control (both grounds are the
  same asset: its `cut_*` terms measure how repeatable a method is). Both grounds are `planar`, so
  Drake's native contact uses its half-space for both and cannot depend on the cut; Chrono's and
  MuJoCo's collide the hulls of the pieces.
- **`wedge`.** A block on an incline is held if and only if tan α < μ; the two blocks sit 10 %
  on either side of the limit. The sliding velocity is as sensitive to μ as in `block`
  (d ln v/d ln μ ≈ −9), so a few percent there is a fraction of a percent in μ.
- **`restitution`.** K = 1e7 makes the impacts last 18 to 30 ms, at least 17 steps of 1 ms. The
  weight is not negligible during them (it lowers the rebound by 2 to 4 %), so the theory is the
  integrated law, class C; the closed form without weight, valid for any elastic f(δ) and any
  mass, h·u_i − ln(1 + h·u_i) = −h·u_o − ln(1 − h·u_o), is the scalar `rebound_<k>_hunt_crossley`
  (0.4284, 0.7484, 1.1873, 1.6429 m/s). The energy term is class A.
- **`oblique`.** The normal impulse comes from the run's own momentum balance, so the theory
  does not need the model's restitution: it tests the friction impulse only.
- **`edge`.** K = 1e7 is the stiffest the explicit coupling takes at 1 ms for this box
  (√(K·A/m)·dt = 0.77). The theory is the rigid one. A Winkler table of this K lets the box lean
  before its centre of mass reaches the edge and pitch 19 % earlier (strip model of the same
  foundation: 0.1 rad at 74.9 ms instead of 92.9 ms; 84.2 ms at K = 1e8, 88.7 ms at 1e9, 90.8 ms at
  1e10). That estimate was made after the first runs showed every method early by the same
  amount; the scenario was left as it was and the prediction added as `pitch_time_<k>_winkler`.
  Read `edge` with a stiffness sweep: at one K it ranks compliance, like `tip`.
- **`turntable`.** The table cannot be driven (the engines take forces, not motions), so it is a
  free 100 kg flywheel on a plane with μ = 1e-5 (it takes 0.1 % of the table's spin in the run;
  zero was the first choice, but Drake's per-geometry friction cannot combine a zero pair with
  the ball's 0.5, and the scenario must run on every engine), and the theory carries its recoil
  exactly. The
  ball starts with the table's spin about the vertical, so that nothing pivots in the theory's
  motion. The Winkler rolling resistance of §4.2 acts here too and is not in the theory.
- **`energy`.** No friction and no damping in any method (native damping 0): whatever energy
  appears or disappears is the discretization's. The metric omits the energy stored in the
  contact spring, which with h = 0 is never damped and varies irregularly with the step and the
  start for every law (`docs/research/anomalies.md` §2): it bounds the drift, it does not measure
  a steady gain or loss.
- **`tower`.** The card asked for ten cubes at 5°. Their resultant would cross the base 0.44 of a
  side off centre: outside the middle third and close to the rigid limit (0.5), where a Winkler
  foundation that is stable at 1 ms (K ≤ 1e7) cannot hold it (it would need K ≥ 4e8). That would
  test stiffness again, as `tip` and `edge` do. Blocks four times wider than tall keep the tilt,
  the number of bodies and the duration, with full contact.
- **`massratio`.** K puts the static penetration of the heavy block at 0.6 mm, under 1 % of its
  height. For the 1 kg block between two such contacts √(2·K·A/m)·dt = 5.7 at 1 ms: an explicit
  coupling is expected to fail at the base step and to hold at 0.2 ms (1.1).
- **Not written: `peg`** (Whitney's jamming and wedging conditions). They are quasi-static
  conditions on an insertion driven through a compliant support; the engines' interface has only
  forces at the centre of mass and no prescribed motion or compliance centre, and I could not
  state a rigorous closed-form result for what the interface can pose.

### 4.4 Independent checks of the theories

During development (2026-11-11) every formula above was re-derived by another route with scripts that
are not kept in the repository; the checks that are kept are `paper/checks/` and the tests.

- Class B: quadrature of the pressure distributions (disk 2/3, 1/2, π/4; ring; cone; bearing
  4/π, 1.371, π/2); the Coulomb disk of `contensou` on a polar grid with `solve_ivp` (0.4518,
  0.5077 and 0.4283 s against 0.452, 0.508 and 0.428 s in the scenario).
- Rolling: the resistance moment by 2-D quadrature of the damped pressure (1/6 and 1/5) and the
  ODE v̇ = a₀ − c·v; the pendulum periods by direct integration against the arithmetic–geometric
  mean (1.07222 s); the decay of the swing; the journal's equilibrium, torque and finite-arc factor.
- `restitution`: the closed form against the integration of m δ̈ = −k δⁿ(1 + hδ̇) for three
  different (k, n, m): the same rebound to five digits; the theory with weight and the spherical
  cap by `solve_ivp` (0.41970, 0.71937, 1.14401, 1.59327 m/s; contact 30 to 18 ms).
- `oblique`: a sphere with a spring-damper normal and regularized Coulomb friction, integrated:
  tangential velocity and rolling speed equal to the impulse formulas to 1e-3 in both regimes.
- `edge`: Newton–Euler with the contact force solved from the constraint, against the Lagrange
  equations used in the scenario: the same pitch to 1e-7; the force on the edge stays positive
  (0.34 to 9.81 N). A separate strip model of the box on a Winkler table gives the convergence
  with K quoted above.
- `turntable`: Newton–Euler of the rolling ball with the friction force solved from the rolling
  constraint: orbit rate (2/7)Ω and constant radius to 1e-8; the recoil enters through the reduced
  mass (equal masses give 4/9).
- `pieces`: for a box sliding without spin at any attitude, the linear pressure that balances the
  friction couple gives no moment about the vertical (1e-17 N·m).
- `wedge`, `tower`, `massratio`: the numbers quoted in the table.
